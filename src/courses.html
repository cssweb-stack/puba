PUBA Courses

Public Administration (PUBA) courses at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa explore how public and nonprofit organizations address community needs. Undergraduate and graduate offerings combine public policy, organizational leadership, and applied learning, with perspectives grounded in Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and beyond.

{% call imageTextRow("/media/units/puba/courses/course-01.webp", 600, 600, "") %}

Upper-Division Courses (300–499)

Upper-division courses introduce the principles and practices of public administration, public policy, and nonprofit leadership. Students explore how public policies are developed, implemented, and evaluated, alongside the organizational structures and management approaches that support effective public service. Courses emphasize research methods, program evaluation, multicultural leadership, and the challenges of managing organizations that serve diverse communities. Through critical analysis, independent inquiry, and opportunities for applied learning, students develop foundational skills in evaluating public problems, understanding organizational responsibilities, and considering strategies for addressing issues of public importance.

{% endcall %} {% call imageTextRow("/media/units/puba/courses/course-02.webp", 600, 600, "") %}

Graduate Foundations (600–609)

Foundational graduate courses provide an introduction to the theories, methods, and professional responsibilities of public administration. Students examine organizational behavior, public budgeting, personnel management, leadership, ethics, and the political and economic forces that influence public institutions. Courses also develop skills in policy analysis, program evaluation, research design, and effective communication across diverse organizational settings. Emphasis is placed on understanding how public and nonprofit organizations operate, how decisions are made, and how resources and responsibilities are managed. These studies establish the analytical and practical foundations for thoughtful leadership and effective public service.

{% endcall %} {% call imageTextRow("/media/units/puba/courses/course-03.webp", 600, 600, "") %}

Specialized Graduate Study and Practice (620–699)

Specialized graduate courses offer opportunities to investigate contemporary challenges in public administration, nonprofit management, and organizational leadership. Students explore public-sector reform, strategic collaboration, policy advocacy, workplace diversity, and the management of organizations serving diverse populations. Courses also examine Indigenous governance, public administration in international contexts, and the distinctive policy and leadership challenges facing Hawaiʻi and the Pacific. Through applied research, professional practicums, and capstone experiences, students connect theory with practice while developing skills in organizational change, program management, and collaborative problem-solving. These experiences prepare students to address complex public issues with ethical judgment, analytical rigor, and a commitment to effective public service.

{% endcall %}
