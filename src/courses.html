Public Administration (PUBA) Courses

Public Administration (PUBA) courses at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa explore how public and nonprofit organizations address community needs. Graduate offerings combine public policy, organizational leadership, and applied learning, with perspectives grounded in Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and beyond.

The information below provides an overview of typical course offerings; specific courses and topics offered vary by semester based on scheduling and instructional resources.

{% call imageTextRow("/media/units/puba/courses/course-02.webp", 600, 600, "", sidebarClass="sidebar-left-sm") %}

Graduate Foundations (600–609)

Foundational graduate courses introduce the theories, methods, and professional responsibilities of public administration. Students examine organizational behavior, public budgeting, personnel management, leadership, ethics, and the political and economic forces that influence public institutions. Courses also develop skills in public management, policy analysis, program evaluation, and research design across diverse organizational settings. The program emphasizes understanding how public and nonprofit organizations operate, how decisions are made, and how resources and responsibilities are managed. These studies establish the analytical and practical foundations for thoughtful leadership and effective public service.

Specialized Graduate Study and Practice (620–699)

Specialized graduate courses offer opportunities to investigate contemporary challenges in public administration, nonprofit management, and organizational leadership. Students explore public-sector reform, strategic collaboration, policy advocacy, workplace diversity, and how to manage organizations that serve diverse populations. Courses also examine Indigenous governance, public administration in international contexts, and the distinctive policy and leadership challenges facing Hawaiʻi and the Pacific. Through applied research, professional practicums, and capstone experiences, students connect theory with practice while developing skills in organizational change, program management, and collaborative problem-solving. These experiences prepare students to address complex public issues with ethical judgment, analytical rigor, and a commitment to effective public service.
